**Linked design:** [46-editor-polish.vscode.md](../../../design/v0.x/46-editor-polish.vscode.md) §17,

## Goals

Reading a long conversation, the same reader named five things: the transcript is too airy,
the state words do not say how a call ended, the dots are small and the rail is drawn over
them, an opened call has no frame around its IN and OUT, and a reply has no ending — the
answer that concludes a turn is indistinguishable from prose in the middle of it.

1. **Moderate density.** The 8px gap and 8px margin around a message stacked into 28px
   between two messages and 20px between a message and a row, and the output caption floated
   a full transcript gap below the row it belongs to. The message's gap and margin become
   4px, a row's summary loses 2px of vertical padding, and the caption is pulled up to sit
   4px under its row.
2. **State words carry their state.** `done` is the green of a successful operation, not
   grey; `stopped` is the warning colour of a run that was interrupted, not italic grey.
3. **The dots are larger and paint over the rail.** A 6px dot under a 1px line reads as a
   bead on a wire; an 8px dot covers it. The dot gains 1px of negative margin on each side,
   so its centre stays at 9px and the row's layout does not move, and a `z-index` so it
   paints above the generated line.
4. **An opened call is framed.** The IN/OUT pane gets a 1px hairline box, a small radius and
   padding, aligned with the row's name; IN and OUT are separated by a hairline rather than
   by 12px of space, and the inner `pre` loses its own left rule, which the frame replaces.
5. **A reply ends in a summary.** When a finished run's last block is prose, that answer
   becomes the turn's summary: an end dot in the ending colour, the word `Summary`, and the
   answer's first line as the title, with the rest of the answer below it. The rail stops at
   the dot rather than running the length of the summary.

## Scope

`editors/vscode/media/chat.css` and `media/chat.js`; `test/unit/chatLayout.test.ts` and a new
`test/unit/summary.test.ts`. The milestone page and the design chapter record the change. No

## Tasks

1. **Tighten the rhythm.** `.msg` gap and margin 4px; the Thinking and tool summaries 4px
   vertical padding; the output caption `margin-top: -8px`; the pane's bottom padding 6px.
   Two messages sit 20px apart, a message and a row 16px, and a row and its caption 4px.
2. **Redraw the joins.** Message rail extensions become 10px, which meets the new 20px
   between messages and over-covers the 16px between a message and a row.
3. **Colour the state words.** `.tool.done .state` in the operation green; `.tool.stopped
   .state` in the warning colour.
4. **Enlarge the dot.** 8px square, `margin: -1px`, `position: relative`, `z-index: 1`, for
   both the tool and the Thinking dot; the open ring becomes 2px so it still reads at the new
   size.
5. **Frame the pane.** `.tool .pane` gets the hairline, radius, 8px 10px padding and 22px
   left margin; `.tool.spawn .pane` opts out; `.tool .section + .section` adds a top hairline
   and 8px; `.tool .section pre` loses its left rule.
6. **End the reply.** `summaryParts(text)` returns the first usable line as a title and the
   rest as the body, or null when the line is empty, a fence, a rule or a table. `markSummary`
   marks a round only when it is the last block of its container and the run finished: it
   replaces the `.who` with end dot, `Summary` and title, re-renders the body without the
   title line, and re-runs the rail pass. The summary's rail segment stops at the dot.
7. **Tests.** Layout assertions for the new spacing, extensions, dot size and stacking,
   colours, frame and summary rail; a `summary.test.ts` for the title, the removed line, the
   fallback and the finished-run rule.
8. **Verify.** Compile, lint and tests; the Go triple from Git Bash; VSIX packaging and the
   packaged smoke test.

## Decisions (before any change)

- **Density is bought with margins, not with the transcript gap.** The 12px between
  transcript children is what separates one step from the next; the excess was the 8px gaps
  and margins stacked around every message and a caption that sat a full gap below its row.
  Reducing those leaves the steps as far apart as before and brings each message's parts —
  the name, the thought, the answer — together.
- **The larger dot keeps its box.** An 8px dot would move the name to 24px and the rail to
  10px. Negative margins make its outer box 6px again: the centre stays at 9px, the name at
  22px, and every inset already written stays true. The dot is bigger; the layout is not.
- **The dot wins with a stacking order, not with a second line.** The rail is generated
  content on the block and paints after the summary; a positioned dot with `z-index: 1` is
  enough to cover it in the same stacking context. Splitting the line around the dot would
  need a second element and would make the markup depend on the dot's position.
- **The summary's title is the answer's own first line.** The model has already written the
  conclusion; a title taken from the prompt or the session would be a second opinion. The
  line is removed from the rendered body so it is said once, and the Copy action still
  carries the whole answer. When the first line is syntax rather than prose — a fence, a
  rule, a table — no summary is drawn at all: a title made of `---` is worse than none.
- **The summary marks a finished run only.** The last text of a failed or stopped run is not
  a conclusion, and calling it one would misreport how the turn ended. Those runs keep the
  existing ending; the state word and the run-end line already say what happened.
- **The frame belongs to a call's own panes.** A spawn card's pane holds the child's whole
  conversation; framing it would put a box around a transcript. The spawn pane opts out and
  keeps its inset and its nesting rule.
- **The rail stops at the end dot.** The dot is the turn's terminal mark; a line continuing
  beside the summary body would make the conclusion read as another step. The summary
  block's segment is the height of its header, and the body sits to the right of nothing.

## Implementation notes

### Density and the rail

`.msg` keeps its column and becomes `gap: 4px; margin-block: 4px`. The transcript's 12px
gap is unchanged, so two messages are 20px apart (12 + 4 + 4) and a message and a row 16px.
The Thinking summary and the tool summary both become `padding: 4px 6px`; the output caption
is a block child of the transcript and gains `margin-top: -8px`, which leaves 4px under its
row. The pane's bottom padding drops from 10px to 6px.

The rail extensions are now 10px on both sides of every block kind: 10 + 10 meets the 20px
between two messages exactly and over-covers the 16px between a message and a row. The output
caption's negative margin only makes its own distance shorter, which the overlapping segments
handle.

### The dots

Both dots are 8px with `margin: -1px`, so the flex line sees a 6px box and the centre remains
at `6px padding + 4px` — the rail's 9px. `position: relative; z-index: 1` puts the dot in
the positive layer of the block's stacking context, above the block's generated `::after`
line. The open and stopped rings grow from 1.5px to 2px.

### The frame

`.tool .pane` gets `margin: 2px 0 6px 22px; padding: 8px 10px; border: 1px solid
var(--ui-hairline); border-radius: var(--ui-radius-sm)`. The pane is a `<details>` child, so
it only renders when the row opens. `.tool.spawn .pane` restores `margin: 0; padding: 0 0 6px
22px; border: 0`. `.tool .section + .section` adds `margin-top: 8px; padding-top: 8px;
border-top: 1px solid var(--ui-hairline)` and `.tool .section pre` drops its `border-left`
and its left padding: the frame is the grouping, and two lines at two distances was the
noise the row grammar removed.

### The summary

`summaryParts(text)` skips leading blank lines, returns null when there is no line, the line
is a fence (` ``` ` / `~~~`), a rule (`---`, `***`, `___`) or a table (`|`), or the line
strips to nothing. Otherwise it strips the heading marks, the blockquote and list markers,
a leading image/link wrapper to its text, inline emphasis and code marks, and a trailing
colon; it clips at 80 characters. The body is every line after the title line, with leading
blank lines removed.

`markSummary(round)` is called by `finishRun` when the failure kind is `none` — see
[p_0.46.17](p_0.46.17.vscode.md), where the first spelling of that test turned out never to run in
a live turn — and by `addRunEnd` for `finished` or no status only, after `endRound`. It refuses
a round whose wrap is not the last transcript
child, ignoring output captions. When it accepts, it replaces the `.who` with a flex row of
end dot, `Summary` and title, adds `.summary`, and re-renders the body from the remainder:
the round's `md` state is reset and `paintMarkdown` runs synchronously, so a queued frame
re-paints the same text rather than restoring the title line. `round.text` is replaced by
the body; the Copy action was already given the whole answer by `endRound`.

The end dot is `--vscode-charts-purple` (fallback `#b180d7`): distinct from the operation
green and the failure red, and not the link blue a path uses. `.msg.assistant.summary::after`
takes a fixed 10px height from its top — 20px when `rail-up` — so the line ends inside the
dot.

## What was not walked

No real window here. The new rhythm — 4px inside a message, 16px and 20px between blocks,
the caption at 4px — was reasoned from the declarations rather than looked at, as were the
8px dot over the 1px line, the frame's weight in the light and high-contrast themes, and a
long title clipping in a narrow panel. The summary block was not rendered: its title
extraction, its body re-render and its rail stop are pinned at the source level only, and
the case where the final answer begins with a code fence is deliberately left without a
summary rather than shown.

## Non-goals

- A setting for density, a collapse-everything control, or a per-tool summary.
- Changing the transcript's 12px gap, the read width, or the text size setting.
- A wrapper element per reply, or a summary generated from the prompt.
- Framing the spawn pane, the approval card, or the user's message.
- A protocol change, a host change, a version bump, a branch, or a push.
